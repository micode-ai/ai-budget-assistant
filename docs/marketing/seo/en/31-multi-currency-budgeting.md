# Multi-Currency Budgeting: Track Money in Two Currencies

You freelance for clients abroad and get paid in dollars or euros, but rent, groceries, and everything else you actually spend on is priced in a different currency where you live. Or you moved for a remote job, your salary lands in your new country's currency, and you still have a loan, a family member, or a flat back home that needs paying in the old one. Either way, it's the same problem: your money sits in two currencies at once, and you don't have one number that tells you what you actually have. On top of that, every conversion you make without thinking, at whatever moment happens to be convenient, quietly costs a bit more than it has to.

## Why doing the math in your head doesn't work

The same instinct behind any real [way to save money](/blog/en/how-to-save-money/), looking at the actual numbers before you decide instead of guessing, applies to currency conversion too, most people just never frame it that way. There are two separate problems here, and they tend to blur together. First, without one combined view of both balances, it's easy to lose track of what you actually have, you're doing the math in your head from whatever rate you last happened to check, and it's already moved by the time you use it. Second, because you can't see the full picture clearly, you convert whenever you need to, not whenever the rate is actually in your favor, and on a recurring transfer even a small difference in rate adds up month after month.

## Your wallet shows a separate balance for each currency on its own

You don't have to manually set up "a euro account" or "a dollar account" in AI Budget Assistant. The moment you have a transaction in a given currency, a separate balance card for it shows up in your wallet automatically, kept apart from the rest. Your balance in dollars stays your balance in dollars, your balance in euros stays your balance in euros, and neither one gets blended into the other into one confusing figure.

## One total, even though your money lives in several currencies

Separate balances matter, but sometimes you just want to know what you have overall, for spending analytics, your budget, or an answer from the AI chat. For that, the app converts everything into one display currency you pick, using the current rate from an external currency data provider, and shows you one consistent number instead of several amounts you'd otherwise have to add up by hand. If a particular rate happens to be briefly unavailable, the total is flagged as approximate rather than pretending to a precision it doesn't have. You can change your display currency at any time in settings, it's just how the total is shown, it doesn't rewrite anything you've already recorded.

## Log the exchange when it actually happens

When you genuinely convert one currency into another, through your bank, a currency exchange service, or a transfer provider, it's worth recording that exchange in [AI Budget Assistant](https://ai-budget.pl), on the Currency Exchange screen under Wallet. Both balances then update by exactly what really happened, not by whatever the app would otherwise have assumed from a general rate. That's the only way both balances actually match reality instead of an estimate.

## Stop checking the rate by hand, set an alert instead

That's where an [exchange rate alert](/blog/en/exchange-rate-alert/) fits in: you pick a currency pair, say EUR to USD, enter a threshold and a direction, and the app checks the rate in the background once an hour against an external currency data provider. Once the threshold is crossed, you get a single push notification, it's not a live rate and not any kind of AI forecast, just a plain number-to-number comparison on a regular interval. The alert is one-shot and tied to you personally, not to a shared account, so if you split an account with someone, their alerts aren't visible to you and yours aren't visible to them. You can have up to twenty active alerts at once, so keeping an eye on more than one pair isn't an issue.

It's worth saying plainly what this doesn't do: it doesn't send or receive actual money across borders. It's a tracking and reminder tool, the actual exchange or transfer still happens through your bank or whichever service you already use.

## Putting it together

Log income and spending in both currencies as they happen, by voice or by photographing a receipt or invoice. Set the display currency you want your combined total shown in. Record the real exchange when it actually occurs instead of letting the app guess a rate on your behalf. And set an alert on whichever pair you convert regularly, so you stop checking out of habit and start acting exactly when it's worth it. If the currency you're holding savings in happens to be losing value faster than it feels like day to day, that's a separate question, worth reading about in our piece on [personal inflation](/blog/en/personal-inflation-rate/).

You can start without a card on file, right in your browser at [ai-budget.pl](https://ai-budget.pl), or install the Android app from [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.budget.assistant).

## FAQ: Multi-Currency Budgeting

**Does the app show a live, real-time exchange rate?**
No. The rate used to convert totals and to check a rate alert comes from an external currency data provider, and the alert specifically checks it once an hour, not continuously. That's enough to catch a good moment to convert, but it isn't a live trading ticker.

**Do I have to manually add a new currency to my wallet before I can use it?**
No. A balance card for a currency appears in your wallet on its own, the moment you have your first transaction in it, there's nothing to set up in advance.

**Can other members of a shared account see my exchange rate alerts?**
No. An exchange rate alert is tied to you as an individual user, not to a shared account, other members don't see your alerts and don't need to set up their own.

**Can the app actually send money abroad or exchange currency for me?**
No. It's a tool for tracking balances, converting totals, and reminding you of a good moment to convert, the exchange or transfer itself still goes through your bank, a currency exchange, or whichever service you already use, and you log what actually happened afterward.
