# Exchange Rate Alert: Get Notified the Moment Your Rate Is Hit

If you regularly convert one currency into another, because you work remotely and get paid in dollars, because you live abroad and send money home every month, or because you are planning a trip and want to exchange a larger sum before you leave, you already know the feeling. You open your banking app or a search bar, check the rate, sigh that it is not quite there yet, and close it. The next day, the same thing. And the day after that.

The problem is not that the rate moves, that is normal. The problem is that the right moment to exchange rarely lines up with the exact moment you happen to have the app open and remember to check. More often it happens in the middle of a workday, overnight, or on a day you simply forgot to look.

## Checking a rate manually is a guessing game

Without something doing this for you, you are left with two options, and neither is great. Check several times a day so you do not miss the window, which gets tiring fast and still gives no guarantee the rate happens to be close to what you want the moment you look. Or check rarely, whenever it crosses your mind, and risk the good moment passing before you notice.

Neither option is particularly rational, it is just what you were left with as long as nobody was doing the watching for you.

## How the exchange rate alert works in AI Budget Assistant

The mechanism is simple to describe. In [AI Budget Assistant](https://ai-budget.pl), under Wallet, on the Currency Exchange screen, you pick a currency pair, say USD to PLN, type in a target rate, and choose a direction: notify me when the rate goes above this number, or notify me when it drops below it. Say you are waiting for the dollar-to-zloty rate to cross 4.20, that is just a hypothetical to illustrate the mechanic, not a forecast or a recommendation of any kind.

From that point on, the app watches the rate for you. In the background, once an hour, the current rate is checked against a live currency data provider. When the condition is actually met, the rate genuinely crosses your threshold in the direction you picked, you get a single push notification. You do not need the app open, you do not need to check anything by hand. Set the alert once and forget about it until your phone tells you it happened.

It is worth being direct about what this is not. It is not a live, second-by-second ticker, and it is not a forecast of where the rate is heading. It is an ordinary, deterministic threshold check against the current market rate, repeated every hour. There is no AI involved, no model predicting future moves, just a plain number-to-number comparison, run on a regular interval.

## What happens once the alert fires

When you get the notification, tapping it takes you straight to the currency exchange screen for that exact pair, ready to go, so the natural next step is to actually record the exchange in the app if you went through with it. There is no hunting for which pair it was or digging back through settings.

One important thing to remember: the alert is one-shot. Once it fires, it is marked as done and will not notify you again, even if the rate stays above (or below) your threshold for days afterward. This is not a recurring reminder that says "the rate is still above X" every so often, it is one specific notification the moment the condition was first met. If you want to be told again after the rate pulls back and later approaches the level you care about, you simply set a new alert with a new target.

## You can watch several pairs at once

An exchange rate alert belongs to you personally, not to a shared account, it is a personal watch rather than something you configure once for the whole family or team. You can have up to twenty active alerts at a time, across as many different currency pairs as you need. Someone paid in euros who spends in zlotys might watch EUR/PLN. Someone planning a trip to the UK might also watch GBP alongside that. There is nothing stopping you from running both at the same time.

A dedicated screen in the app shows every alert you have set, in one place, both the ones still waiting to trigger and the history of ones that already fired, together with the rate and the moment it happened. You do not need to open the specific currency pair's screen to see what is going on elsewhere, the whole overview is available from one place.

## Who this genuinely helps

This feature is most useful for three kinds of situations. First, freelancers and remote workers paid in one currency who spend day-to-day in another, for them a better rate at the moment of conversion translates directly into more money staying in their pocket. Second, people living abroad who regularly send larger sums back home or the other way, at larger amounts even a small difference in the rate matters, so it is worth choosing the moment deliberately instead of exchanging whenever it happens to fall on the calendar. Third, travelers who plan to exchange a larger sum before a trip and would rather wait for a better moment than convert under time pressure at the airport or right before departure.

In all three cases the point is the same: stop checking the rate out of habit and start reacting exactly when it is worth reacting.

## FAQ

**Does the exchange rate alert show a live, real-time rate?**
No. The rate is checked once an hour against a live currency data provider, it is not updated live second by second. That is enough to catch the moment your threshold is crossed, but it is not a live trading ticker.

**Will I get notified every hour the rate stays above my target?**
No, the alert is one-shot. The first time the condition is met, you get a single notification and the alert is marked as triggered. If you want to be told again in the future, you set a new alert with a new target.

**Does the alert use AI to predict where the rate is going?**
No. It is a plain, deterministic comparison between the current rate and the threshold you set, checked hourly. There is no forecast and no model predicting future movement, AI Budget Assistant has other AI-powered features, but this is not one of them.

**How many alerts can I have at once, and do they work on a shared account?**
You can have up to twenty active alerts at a time, across any number of currency pairs. It is a personal setting tied to your own user account, not to a shared family or business account, other members of a shared account do not see your alerts or need to set up their own.
